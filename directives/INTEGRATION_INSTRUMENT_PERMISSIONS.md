## 2. What every letter has to say, and why

Three things, none of them optional.

1. **That the use is commercial.** Say it in the first paragraph, in those words. Most of these instruments are free for research, and permission granted by someone who believed this was a study is not permission. This is the single way to turn a yes into a liability later.
2. **That the item text will be unchanged and cited.** It is the project's own hard rule anyway ([DECODED_SCORING.md](DECODED_SCORING.md)) — changing item wording invalidates stored responses and forces retakes — so the commitment costs nothing and it is the thing rights-holders actually care about.
3. **What the user will and will not be shown.** Scored but never presented as an assessment; no disease names, no severity bands, no clinical thresholds, no percentiles (I7.6, and California AB 489's fact pattern). A rights-holder's real fear is their instrument reappearing as a diagnosis in a consumer app. Saying plainly that it will not is both true and the strongest thing in the letter.

And one thing not to do: **do not ask for more than you need.** No exclusivity, no redistribution rights, no derivative works. A narrow ask gets answered; a broad one gets referred to a university licensing office and disappears.

## 3. The letters

### 3.1 ISLES-SF — send first

> **Subject:** Permission to use ISLES-SF in a commercial self-reflection tool
>
> Dear Dr —,
>
> I'm writing to ask permission to use the ISLES-SF in a commercial product. I want to be direct about that up front: this is not a research study. It is a paid coaching product for people trying to make sense of a life-altering experience.
>
> The context: many of them have had an experience — a near-death episode, a bereavement, a sudden collapse of a belief they had built a life on — that they cannot fit into the story they had. What they describe is exactly the loss of footing your scale measures, and "footing" is the word they use themselves.
>
> What I would like to administer is the six-item short form, both factors, **with the item wording unchanged and the scale cited to you by name** in the product itself.
>
> How the results are used matters, so I want to be specific. The two factor scores are shown back to the person as a starting point for a conversation. They are not presented as an assessment, and there are no clinical bands, thresholds, cut-offs, disease names or percentile ranks anywhere in the product. Nothing is diagnosed and nothing is treated. If a score suggests someone is having a hard time, the product's response is to slow down and, where appropriate, point them toward a licensed professional — never to interpret the number for them.
>
> If there is a licensing route or a fee for commercial use, I would rather pay it than proceed on an informal yes. And if the answer is no, I would be grateful to know that too, so I can design without it rather than around it.
>
> Thank you for the instrument, and for your time.
>
> Thomas Wood

### 3.2 Core Beliefs Inventory — send first

> **Subject:** Permission to use the Core Beliefs Inventory commercially
>
> Dear —,
>
> I'd like to ask permission to use the Core Beliefs Inventory (Cann et al., 2010) in a commercial product — a paid coaching tool, not a research study.
>
> The users are people whose experience forced a re-examination of things they had taken as settled. The CBI is the closest thing I have found to the question the product is actually asking, which is not *how much damage was done* but *how much had to be reconsidered*. That distinction is the whole design: a high score is presented to the user as a reason the difficulty makes sense, never as a measure of harm.
>
> I would administer the nine items **with wording unchanged and the inventory cited to the authors** in the product.
>
> On presentation: the score is shown as a single dial with a plain-language description and no clinical framing. There are no bands, thresholds, disease names or percentiles anywhere in the product, and nothing is diagnosed or treated. Post-traumatic growth language is deliberately withheld from users in the early weeks, because for this population it reads as an instruction to feel better rather than as a finding.
>
> If commercial use requires a licence or fee, I'd rather arrange it properly than rely on an informal yes.
>
> Thank you,
>
> Thomas Wood
> tom@masterytv.com

### 3.4 NDE-C — add-on

>
> Dear Dr —,
>
> I'd like to ask about terms for using the NDE-C in a commercial product — a paid coaching tool for people integrating an experience, not a research study.
>
> I want to be clear about what it would and would not be used for. It would **not** be used as a gate: nobody is told they did or did not have a near-death experience, and no score qualifies or disqualifies anyone from anything. The older scale's affective items are uniformly positively valenced, so a purely distressing experience scores low on the field's own instrument, and distressing experiences are a substantial part of who this product is for.

> Items would be **offered rather than required**, wording unchanged, scale cited. No bands, thresholds, diagnosis or percentiles anywhere.

## 4. If the answer is no

Decide this before sending, so a refusal is a design change rather than a negotiation.

- **ISLES-SF refused** → the primary outcome measure is gone and there is no clean substitute. I7 would ship on the CBI alone, with FIT and FOOTING dropped from The Map (§5.5's table becomes one dial). Worth a second, more specific ask before accepting.
- **CBI refused** → THE SHAKE is gone, which is the dial the coaching question is built on. Same: ask again before redesigning.
- **Any add-on refused** → drop that lens's instrument. The add-ons are already optional and post-Company, so nothing structural breaks; the lens simply stops having an instrument attached.
- **No reply within three weeks** → treat as a no and design without it. A silent maybe is the worst input to a build, and the rule in §2 is written so that silence has a defined consequence.

Whatever comes back, record it in §1 and update the §2 row and the [INTEGRATION_SPRINT.md](INTEGRATION_SPRINT.md) §5 status table. If an instrument is dropped, it also comes out of I7.1/I7.2, EXPERIENCE §5.5's table, and the `BATTERIES` entry in I7.4.
